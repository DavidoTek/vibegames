Rail Dispatcher

Build a railway, set up lines and watch the automatic dispatcher route trains through it. Find out where your network runs out of capacity.

Building

  • Track: drag from one grid point to another. The track runs straight and then at 45° (start the drag diagonally to get diagonal first). Trains can turn at most 45° at a point, so where several tracks meet at such angles you get a switch.
  • Station: drag along existing track to turn it into a platform. Platforms close to an existing station join it, otherwise a new station is founded. Each platform track can hold one train.
  • Signal: tap the track just before the point where trains should stop. Tap again to make it one-way (ringed lamp, trains may not pass it from behind), a third tap removes it.
  • Erase: tap or drag over track.

How trains run

Before moving, a train reserves its route up to the next signal facing it, or all the way to its platform if there is none. Reserved track is shown in the line colour and nobody else can use it, so trains never collide; more signals mean more trains can share a line. Trains pick free platforms and reverse at the end of a line by themselves.

Avoid signals in the middle of single-track sections: two trains can meet there and get stuck. A train stuck for 10 game minutes is towed away and counted as a deadlock.

Lines & capacity

In the Lines panel add stops (tap stations on the map), choose a train type and the number of trains. Analyze capacity simulates the map with 1 to 8 trains on that line and shows where more trains stop helping. Usage colours tracks by how often they are reserved, and the Stats tab lists the signals where trains wait most: your bottlenecks.

Controls

Drag to pan (two fingers on touch, or right/middle mouse in any tool), wheel or pinch to zoom. Keys: 1–5 tools, Space pause, U usage, F fit, Ctrl+Z undo, Esc cancel.
